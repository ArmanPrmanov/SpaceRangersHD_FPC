human's mid-day dialogue pauses the host's day and is answered on the client
(not auto-answered); hosting and joining get in-game menus as well as flags.

## UI (the game's screens)

- Done:
  - `source/screens/NetPlay.pas`: `--host=PORT` hosts the running game from its
    first game screen; `--join=HOST:PORT --name=NAME` joins after the startup
    load, applies the whole state and opens the human's screen. Refusals, a lost
    host and a state that does not come go to the main menu with a message.
  - Pumping: every message-loop iteration (`GR_Main.FramePumpHook`), in
    `WaitForTurnCalculationUI` (`aCalc.TurnWaitPumpHook`) and in the star map's
    waits for a job or a talk (10 ms loops; the shim's handles cannot wait on
    `NetInboxEvent`). The host's pump takes no new peer and sends no status
    while a job runs.
  - Host: every end-turn site asks `NetEndTurn`: the last day's state goes if
    it has not, a modal box shows who the day waits for, then the queues apply
    and `BeginDay` precedes the player-star job. Films go as soon as the day has
    run. The state goes at the quiet point (the star map after its preparation,
    other screens after the galaxy job). Continuing travel starts the next day at
    the film's end, after the peers.
  - Client: end turn sends `EndTurn` and waits (modal, the host's `Status`).
    In space or taking off it plays the host's film of its star on the star map,
    bound to its scene; at the film's end, and otherwise when the state comes,
    the screen closes and the state is applied between screens, where the
    end-of-film steps (landing wear, black-hole scripts) run as commands and the
    planet, station, jump screen or star map opens. Travel that goes on ends the
    next turn by itself. In hyperspace the jump screen applies each state.
    `ThreadCalc.TurnJobsForbidden` makes any turn job on a client raise.
  - A disconnected human holds position: `HoldPosition` (kind 46) also ends
    automatic control.
- Deviations from the design, recorded in docs/network.md: the client never
  sends `ContinueUntilArrival` (it ends each travelling day itself); films
  leave during the galaxy job; the arrival film from hyperspace and the old
  arcade/Terron battles are not shown on a client; waits are timeout loops.
- Hand check (two game processes, each with its own `--user-dir`; the host with
  `--host=0 --load-save=<save>`, the client with `--join=127.0.0.1:<port from
  the host's Rangers.log> --name=Guest`):
  1. The client opens on a planet of a coalition star with a new ranger named
     Guest; the host's log says the human joined, the client's says the state
     was applied with no desync.
  2. The host ends its turn first: a box says it waits for Guest. The client
     ends its turn: the host's box closes and the day runs on the host.
  3. The client ends its turn first: its box says it waits for the host's
     human, then that the day runs; it then opens the planet again, one day on.
  4. Client takes off (hangar, uninhabited planet, station): the star map opens
     and plays the takeoff film, then order mode on the new state.
  5. Client in space: the film shows the host's day of its star, ships and
     shots moving where they end up; after it the map is centred on its ship.
  6. Multi-day travel on the client: days go on without the end-turn button,
     until arrival, a fight or the break button.
  7. Client lands: the film ends with the landing and the planet screen opens;
     on an uninhabited planet the engine wears (on the host with the next
     day's commands).
  8. Client jumps: the jump screen stays until arrival (the host runs the days
     without waiting for it), then the star map of the new star.
  9. Host continuing travel: its film ends, the box waits for the client, then
     the next film plays.
  10. Close the client: the host's log says the human disconnected, its ranger
      stops and does not fly on under automatic control; the host's days no
      longer wait. Rejoin with the same name: the same ranger comes back.
  11. Close the host while the client waits: the client goes to the main menu
      and says the host left.
  12. A game without `--host`/`--join` plays as before (end turn, film, landing,
      hot-seat with `--hotseat=2`).

## Remote dialogue

- Done:
  - The conversation machinery addresses the human asked: `ShowPlayerDialogue`
    and `OpenPlayerConversation` take that human's ranger, the talk fields,
    `TalkResponse` and `PlayerDialogueRequestCount` are that human's, and
    `aHuman.CanAskHuman` decides who can be asked (the local human, or a
    connected remote one where a session asks them). Single player takes the
    same path as before. The machinery, host, client, replay and markers are
    617ddd8; the headless probe and the tests the next commit.
  - Host: the turn thread calls `RemoteTalkHook` (`TNetHost.RemoteTalk`) inside
    the talk's wait. It sends `TalkRequest`, reads only that peer's inbox, runs
    each command of the human through `SubmitCommand` (recorded, actor checked)
    and returns on `TalkDone`, `Bye`, a closed connection, the host stopping, or
    10 minutes, which it logs. The pump leaves that inbox alone and takes what
    the turn thread deferred afterwards. `ProtocolVersion` is 2.
  - Client: `WaitModal` (and so the wait at the film's end) takes a
    `TalkRequest`: the box closes, the star map's talk screen opens for the
    ship, its answers go to the host as commands, `TalkDone` follows, the wait
    opens again. A talk it cannot show is declined at once.
  - Replay: the recorded `sqRemoteTalk` answers say who was asked and whether
    the talk ended with `TalkDone`; the replay's own `RemoteTalkHook` has
    `ReplayJob` run the recorded commands as a local talk's.
  - Markers: the NPC offers and demands (extortion of money and cargo, truce
    offer, attack request in `aRanger`; `TryExtortShip` and the attack request
    in `aShip`; the seven partner complaints) go to a remote human. A script's
    dialogue (`SF_Dialog`, `TPlanet.RequestDialog`), the queued arcade and
    planet battles, `EnterRuinsMode`/`CloseRuinsModeScreen` and deploying a
    tranclucator while docked stay with the local human, commented with why.
    No `HUMANS:` marker about dialogues is left; `tools/player_sites.txt`
    updated (unclassified `GetPlayer` sites went down by four).
  - Tests: `--sim-net-talk=DAY` (host and replay) asks the remote humans of
    one star a truce offer; `--sim-net-talk-leave` makes a client leave in the
    talk. `tools/test.py` checks, for 2 and 8 humans, that each asked client
    answered, the host saw `response=1`, the hashes match, the host's log
    replays with the same talks; for 2 humans, that a client leaving in a talk
    does not hang the host and gets its human back. A unit test round-trips
    `TalkRequest`.
- Deviations, recorded in docs/network.md: a talk that ends without
  `TalkDone` ends as one the screen could not show (answer 0), not as
  automatic control, since the NPC's side is already decided then; script
  dialogues stay local; the headless client and the regular networked test
  now answer every NPC offer to a remote human by agreeing.
- Not run: at the owner's request no game process was started in this
  package, so the baseline and final `tools/test.py` runs, the save suite and
  the two-process smoke test are owed; the code builds and the unit tests
  (433 checks), `check_layers.py` and `player_sites.py --check` pass.
- Hand check (two game processes as in the UI section):
  1. The client's ranger in space in the host's star, an NPC pirate there:
     both end the turn. The pirate's demand opens the talk screen on the
     client over its star map; the host's box keeps waiting, and its log says
     `talk with human N`.
  2. Pay on the client: the host's log says `ended: answered, K commands,

  3. Refuse: response 0; the pirate attacks in the film.
  4. Trade from the talk screen (a ship that offers it): the goods shop opens

  5. Close the client in the talk: the host's day goes on (`ended: left` or

  6. A client landed or on its planet screen when asked: the host's log says

  7. Record the host (`--record-commands`) through a remote talk and replay it

  8. Single player and hot-seat: an NPC's demand opens the talk as before.
- Known gaps: the NPC's line is in the host's language; the other clients'
